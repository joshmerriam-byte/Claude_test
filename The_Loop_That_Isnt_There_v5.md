# The Loop That Isn't There v5

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## \[INTRO -- SCENE\]

ALEX (Pronounce Urumau to rhyme with toe, spoken starting in an upbeat tone, shifting to softly descriptive): One of my favourite views in Lyttelton, is from the top of the Port Saddle, above Urumau Reserve.  (pause)  Off the backside, you can see right out the mouth of the harbour, to the open ocean.

JAMIE (warm, conversational): Yeah. It's a good climb to earn that view.

ALEX (slight pause, thoughtful): On a clear day it feels... bigger than
it should.

JAMIE (curious, leaning in): Bigger how?

ALEX (gentle, searching for words): Um... exposed. Windy. Quiet in that
high-up way. Like you're standing on the edge of something.

ALEX (settling into narrative): It's popular --- for good reason.

ALEX (steady, descriptive): Locals walk up from the neighbourhoods
below, through the regenerating natives.

ALEX (slightly upbeat): Keen mountain bikers grind up the same line, a
bit out of breath, before dropping down the backside.

JAMIE (light, amused): Everyone arrives slightly humbled.

ALEX (small laugh in voice): Yeah. Pretty much.

ALEX (lowering tone, introducing tension): But there's a line back there
that doesn't quite connect.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## \[ACT I -- THE MISSING PIECE\]

JAMIE (genuinely curious): What do you mean?

ALEX (clear, explanatory): Right now, if you're walking Urumau, you
either go out and back...

ALEX (slight emphasis): Or you end up stepping onto sections of downhill
mountain bike track to make a loop.

JAMIE (measured concern): And that's where the tension shows up.

ALEX (agreeing, calm): Not dramatic tension. Just... friction.

ALEX (matter-of-fact): Walkers and descending riders move at very
different speeds.

JAMIE (probing): Different expectations too, eh?

ALEX (gentle affirmation): Yeah. Exactly.

ALEX (structured, explanatory): Since 2018, there's been a proposal to
add a short connection around the backside.

ALEX (clarifying, steady): It would let walkers complete a proper
circuit, climb back to the saddle, and return via the existing Korimako
track.

JAMIE (thinking aloud): So fewer shared downhill sections.

ALEX (firm but calm): That's the idea. Cleaner separation. Better flow.

JAMIE (slightly skeptical): And riders? Be honest.

ALEX (open, transparent): Riders who want another lap currently have to
traverse the hillside all the way back to the front just to climb again.

ALEX (balanced tone): This connection would let them ride back up from
the backside.

ALEX (shrug in voice): It might be a bit easier. Might not. That part's
secondary.

ALEX (grounded): The real point is connectivity.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## \[ACT II -- THE NO\]

JAMIE (direct, serious): So why isn't it there?

ALEX (plainspoken): Because that section of land belongs to the
Lyttelton Port Company.

ALEX (slight emphasis): And they've said no.

JAMIE (measured): Just no?

ALEX (steady): For now, yes.

ALEX (calm explanation): The proposal was consulted on. Approved in
principle by the Community Board.

ALEX (slight pause): But without landowner permission, it stops there.

JAMIE (building tension): And yet Josh Merriam --- and the local MTB
community --- and the reserves committee he's part of --- are still
thinking about it.

ALEX (careful, deliberate): They are. Not defiantly.

ALEX (slight pause): Persistently.

JAMIE (sharper challenge): Is that appropriate?

ALEX (thoughtful, measured): That's the right question.

JAMIE (pressing, but fair): If the landowner says no, at what point does
continuing become entitlement?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## \[ACT III -- THE LINE BETWEEN\]

ALEX (slow, reflective): There's a line there. And yeah... it matters.

ALEX (clear, careful): If advocacy turns into disregard for ownership,
that's wrong.

ALEX (slight hesitation, human): I mean --- that's just basic
stewardship.

JAMIE (softening, exploring): So what makes this different?

ALEX (measured): Posture.

ALEX (steady, thoughtful): There's a difference between "we deserve
this"... and "we think this could serve the wider community."

JAMIE (considering): And you keep making that case.

ALEX (gentle affirmation): Patiently. Publicly. Through formal channels.

JAMIE (analytical): So the long game isn't pressure.

ALEX (calm): It's credibility.

JAMIE (reflective): Trust builds slowly.

ALEX (agreeing): Yeah. Slowly.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## \[ACT IV -- TIME\]

JAMIE (curious): You mentioned Josh once framing this personally.

ALEX (warm, reflective): When the current triennium began, his daughter
was an infant.

ALEX (steady): He said he'd be disappointed if she was old enough to
ride confidently in the reserve and the loop still didn't exist.

JAMIE (softly): That's a long horizon.

ALEX (gentle): Local projects don't always move at electoral speed.

ALEX (slight emphasis): They move at generational speed.

JAMIE (thoughtful): That changes the stakes.

ALEX (quiet conviction): Yeah. It does.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## \[ACT V -- SMALL GOODS\]

JAMIE (balanced, skeptical but fair): Some people will say this is
niche. A handful of riders wanting one more lap.

ALEX (honest, open): It would be easy to frame it that way.

ALEX (slight lift in voice): But trails shape culture.

ALEX (measured): They shape how kids understand the hills around them.

JAMIE (curious): You keep saying "small goods."

ALEX (gentle emphasis): Small goods matter.

ALEX (reflective): A local loop won't transform the economy.

ALEX (warm): But it might transform a Saturday.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## \[ACT VI -- ACCEPTING NO\]

JAMIE (quiet, serious): Let's say the answer never changes.

ALEX (steady, calm): Then that's the answer.

(pause --- 2 seconds)

ALEX (measured, reflective): Advocacy only works if you're prepared to
accept a genuine no after due process.

ALEX (firm but gentle): Otherwise it curdles.

JAMIE (softly): So you ride the long way around.

ALEX (calm, resolved): Yeah.

ALEX (final, reflective, understated): In the meantime... we ride the
long way around.
